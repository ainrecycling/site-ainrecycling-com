Recognising carbide in 3 tests

In a workshop, carbide and steel get mixed up quickly. Three one-minute tests are enough to tell them apart, without tools.

By Malik Gülseven, updated on 9 November 2026

In short

  • Carbide weighs about twice as much as steel.
  • It barely sticks to a magnet.
  • Look for HM, VHM or K10 engraved on the tool.

1. The weight

Tungsten carbide is about twice as dense as steel: 14 to 15 g/cm³, against 7.8 for steel. Pick up two tools of the same size: the carbide one feels much heavier in the hand.

2. The magnet

A magnet sticks hard to high-speed steel (HSS) and tool steel. It barely holds carbide, because of the little cobalt it contains. Whatever sticks hard goes to one side.

3. The markings

  • On the shank of a drill or end mill: “HM” or “VHM” (Hartmetall, Vollhartmetall), “carbide” or “solid carbide”.
  • On a box of inserts: an ISO grade such as P25, M20 or K10.
  • “HSS”, “HSS-E” or “HSS-Co”: that is high-speed steel, not carbide.

The tricky cases

  • Ceramic and cermet inserts have the same shape and ISO codes (CNMG…) as carbide, but they are much lighter. We do not buy them.
  • Brazed tools have a carbide tip brazed onto a steel body: assessed case by case.
  • Coatings in gold, black or blue change nothing: it is coated carbide, bought at the same price.
